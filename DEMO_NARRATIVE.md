## The narrative — what to say while you type

### Beat 0 — The setup line (5 seconds)
> *"This is the Reynolds family. Tom is 68, recently discharged from heart-failure surgery. His mother Helen is 84, early dementia. Sarah is the daughter — she's the caregiver for both. Right now Anchor says everyone is calm."*

(Point at the three green rows. The tagline "The intelligent layer that was always missing" sits at the top.)

### Beat 1 — Single observation, dashboard already responds
> *"Sarah notices Tom's ankles look a bit off. She tells Anchor in plain English."*

**Type into chat:** `Tom's ankles look a little puffy today`

→ Tom's row drops **97 → 81**. Still GREEN. Sparkline visibly bends down on the right.

> *"Notice the score moved, but no alarm. Anchor is logging it against the Heart Failure Symptom Monitoring Framework — a peer-reviewed instrument from Georgetown / NIH. One signal isn't enough to raise anything yet."*

### Beat 2 — Second signal, state changes
**Type:** `He hardly touched dinner, said he wasn't hungry`

→ Tom's row drops **81 → 74 (YELLOW · "Worth watching")**. Sparkline keeps descending.

> *"Now we have two of the eight HF symptom domains active. Anchor moved Tom from CALM to WATCH. Still no alert — the framework says watch carefully, don't intervene."*

### Beat 3 — Safety signal stacks
**Type:** `He forgot to take his evening blood thinner`

→ Tom's row stays **74 (YELLOW)** but the **agent-reasoning ribbon at the bottom** shows the high-risk-combo fire.

> *"Edema plus a missed anticoagulant — that's the high-risk combination the framework flags specifically. Anchor noticed. Not loud yet, but tracked."*

### Beat 4 — The state crosses
**Type:** `His ankles are really swollen now, he just doesn't seem himself`

→ Tom's row drops **74 → 49 (AMBER · "Worth raising soon")**. Below the rows, a **PatternAlertCard** appears: *"A pattern worth raising with Tom's cardiologist"* — with the WHY ("4 symptom domains active at moderate-or-worse severity in 7-day window; high-risk combo flagged"), 3 NEXT STEPS, and a quiet citation footer.

> *"There it is. Four observations from Sarah, in plain English, and Anchor has built — in real time — a card she can take to Tom's cardiologist appointment. Not a diagnosis. Not advice. Just the pattern, the evidence, and the framework citation. The card itself is **generated by the agent** — the layout, the next steps, the talking points — every render is custom-built per situation."*

### Beat 5 — The Sarah moment (the emotional beat)
> *"While all that's happening, Sarah is also being watched — but for a different thing. The Zarit Burden Inventory tracks caregiver wellbeing. Watch what happens when she types something that sounds like a sigh."*

**Type:** `I really don't know how much longer I can do this`

→ Sarah's row drops **97 → 42 (AMBER)**. A new card appears: *"This phrase is a validated early indicator of caregiver depression."*

> *"That single sentence triggered the **ZBI Z10 hopelessness override** — a clinically-validated rule that says: regardless of whether someone scores high overall, this specific phrase warrants attention. Most caregiver tools would have missed this because her cumulative score was still low. Anchor caught it because it understands the instrument, not just the math."*

### Beat 6 — Show the trigger button moment (optional, if time permits)
> *"And just to show the agent isn't only reactive — let me show what happens when multiple signals arrive at once."*

Click the **"Helen · silent decline"** button in the trigger row.

→ The dashboard rebuilds: Helen's score crashes, a **ContributorMap** appears showing 4 different observers (Sarah, Tom, Emma the granddaughter, Mrs Chen the neighbour) all noticed something in the same week — when normally Helen has 1 observation/month. *9× the normal rate.*

> *"This is the multi-observer story. Helen's individual signals were each mild. But the **rate of change** is what the NPI — the Neuropsychiatric Inventory — flags as 'rapid acceleration'. Four people noticed something in one week. That's the pattern. Anchor surfaced it. Sarah didn't have to."*

### Beat 7 — Land it
> *"That's Anchor. Three peer-reviewed instruments. One agent. UI that builds itself from what the data needs. Not chat-with-RAG. Not a dashboard with hardcoded panels. **Generative UI** — the layout is decided by the agent at runtime."*

(Pause. Smile. Stop talking.)

## What if something goes wrong

### "I typed something and nothing changed"
The parser missed it. **Retype using one of the exact phrasings from the table at the top.** Don't improvise — the parser is pattern-based and "haven't been sleeping well" works but "didn't get much rest" might not.

### "The chat says 'I couldn't tell who that was about'"
You forgot the person's name. Use **"Tom"**, **"Helen"**, or **"Mom"** in the message. Or open the chat and the inferred-person fallback will route to the most recent context.

### "The score went the wrong direction"
Click **Reset**. Start over. Don't try to debug live.

### "Nothing is loading"
- Check `http://localhost:8000/health` returns OK
- Check the green **"Live"** pill in the top right of the page
- Refresh the browser

| `forgot to take his thinner` | `S7_missed_medication` | moderate (2) | n/a | HF Framework |
| `really swollen` | `S3_edema` (severe 3) + `S5_general_unwellness` | severe (3) | n/a | HF Framework |
| `same question four times` | `C1_memory_repetition` | moderate (2) | repeat (2) | NPI |

| `left the stove on` | `C3_safety_failure` | severe (3) | repeat (2) | NPI |

| `feel completely alone` | `Z3_isolation` | moderate (2) | n/a | ZBI-12 |
| `snapped at... feel terrible` | `Z4_guilt` | moderate (2) | n/a | ZBI-12 |

## What to skip

- **Don't open the "Agent reasoning" panel** unless a judge asks. It's there for credibility but it's tech-speak.
- **Don't click `?dev=1`** or mention it. Layout codes (`single_alert`, `combined_triage`, `dual_risk`) are internal vocabulary.
- **Don't say "GPT" or "LLM"**. Say "the agent". The agent is doing model-grounded reasoning over peer-reviewed instruments — not playing word association.
